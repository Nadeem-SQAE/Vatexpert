US Sales Tax vs VAT — Key Differences Explained

📅 February 2025 · ⏱ 6 min read · ✍️ VATCalc Team

The United States is one of the few major economies that does not use a national Value Added Tax (VAT) system. Instead, the US uses Sales Tax — a system that operates very differently from VAT and varies significantly from state to state, county to county, and even city to city.

Understanding the difference between US Sales Tax and VAT is essential for international businesses selling to US customers, and for Americans doing business with VAT-registered companies abroad.

Key Differences: VAT vs US Sales Tax

Feature VAT (Most Countries) US Sales Tax
Where collected Every stage of supply chain Final point of sale only
Who collects All VAT-registered businesses Retailers at point of sale
Rate setting National government State + county + city
Number of rates 1–4 rates per country 10,000+ rate combinations in US
B2B treatment Pay VAT, reclaim via input credit Exempt with resale certificate
Price display Usually included in displayed price Added at checkout
Federal level Yes — one national rate No — no federal Sales Tax
Range 5% (UAE/Canada) to 27% (Hungary) 0% (Oregon) to ~13.5% (Louisiana combined)
Compliance Register once per country May need to register in every state where you have 'nexus'

Sales Tax Nexus — What Online Sellers Need to Know

Since the 2018 Supreme Court ruling in South Dakota v. Wayfair, US states can require out-of-state online sellers to collect Sales Tax even without a physical presence in the state — as long as they exceed an "economic nexus" threshold.

Most states set the threshold at $100,000 in sales or 200 transactions per year. This means online businesses selling across the US may need to collect and remit Sales Tax in multiple states — a significant compliance burden compared to the EU's single VAT registration system.

B2B Sales Tax Exemptions

In the US, businesses buying goods for resale can provide a resale certificate to avoid paying Sales Tax. The seller must keep this certificate on file. This is different from VAT, where businesses pay VAT and then reclaim it as input tax.
